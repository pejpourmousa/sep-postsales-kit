2 · Turn activity into hours (the time model)

Few systems log time directly, so each activity type gets a duration: measured where the system records it, standard minutes where it doesn't. Keep the table in one config sheet so RevOps can tune it.

CategoryRuleMinutes
■ Customer meetingsCalendar duration where 1+ attendee has a customer domainactual
■ CallsDialer talk time + wrap-up; a no-connect attempt counts as 2 mintalk + 3
■ EmailPer customer email sent; replies received counted at 1 min4
■ Playbook tasksPrep and follow-up by task template (for example, Business Review = 90, No-contact trigger = 10). The customer touch itself is counted as the call, email or meeting, so nothing is counted twice.by template
■ Internal meetingsCalendar duration where all attendees share the company domainactual
■ Admin & CRMField edits × 1 min, notes × 3, internal Slack threads × 2derived
Capacity(Working days − PTO) × 8 h480 / day

Customer-facing % = meetings + calls + email ÷ all logged hours. Utilization = logged hours ÷ capacity. Anything under ~80% utilization usually means activity isn't being captured, not that the rep is idle.

3 · Build steps

  1. Lock the taxonomy. Agree the six categories, the customer vs internal domain rule, and which roles count (AM, CSM, Onboarding).
  2. Pull 13 weeks of raw activity from each source and match every row to an account (by domain) and an owner (by user ID).
  3. Apply the time model and write one fact table with one row per rep, week and category, using the schema below.
  4. Join account snapshots (ARR, health, renewal date, last successful touch) to find accounts with no connected call, replied email or held meeting in 90 days.
  5. Publish. Build the views in Salesforce reports or CRM Analytics, in a warehouse + BI tool (Snowflake/dbt → Looker, Sigma), or export the CSVs and have Claude regenerate this page from them.
  6. Set the cadence. Managers review the scorecard weekly in 1:1s; leadership reviews coverage gap and cost to serve monthly.
